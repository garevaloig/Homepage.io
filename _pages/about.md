I am a quantitative social scientist with a focus on comparative social policy, public opinion and political participation.

Current research: The political participation of care workers.
======

Since November 2025, I have been a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Amsterdam’s Department of Political Science, working in the NWO-VIDI–funded project “Care Politics" with Dr. Franca van Hooren. 

Care work constitutes an important foundation of individual and societal welfare, yet many care workers in Europe endure highly precarious working conditions. Political participation constitutes an important channel to improve such conditions, yet we know very little about care workers' political participation. Within the "Care Politics" project, we aim to understand the ways in which workers in the broader European care sector become politically active and engage in participation forms such as voting, unionising, joining a political party, or participating in public protests. We combine cross-national and longitudinal survey data with qualitative interviews, document analysis, and parliamentary debates to study how care workers' political participation opportunities are shaped by institutional designs, workforce composition traits, and internal dualisation within the care work market, among other factors.

Doctoral research: Welfare familialism, family solidarity and welfare preferences.
======
Between 2021 and 2025, I completed my PhD at the Bremen International Graduate School of Social Sciences, University of Bremen.

My dissertation 'Familialism Begets Familialism? How Family Solidarity Shapes Welfare Preferences in Europe', explores how family structures and patterns of familial support influence individuals' preferences for welfare policies. 
Specifically, it investigates the following questions:

- In what ways does receiving family support for social risks—such as youth unemployment or the need for elderly care—shape individuals' willingness to endorse public policies addressing the same risks?
- Conversely, how does providing such support within families influence attitudes toward state intervention in these areas?
- How do social policies differentiate between family types in distributing benefits, services, and tax responsibilities, and what impact does this have on public support for these policies?
- How do cross-national differences in social policy and family norms influence the dynamics described above?

To address these questions, I analysed cross-national survey data within the European context, drawing on sources such as the European Social Survey and the Generations and Gender Survey.

I defended my dissertation in October 2025, receiving a 'pass with distinction'. The dissertation was supervised by Prof. Dr. Patrick Sachweh, Dr. Nate Breznau and Prof. Dr. Manuela Naldini.
